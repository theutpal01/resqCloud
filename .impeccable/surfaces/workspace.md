# ResQCloud operational workspace

Scope: Shared signed-in workspace chrome and operational overview, with public reporting and tracking still available. Visitor mode: operate.

Audience and job: Dispatchers and supervisors need a trustworthy view of incidents in their authorized scope and a clear next action. Responders need their assigned work; citizens need their own reports and public routes. Preserve role and department boundaries in navigation and direct-route handling.

Task and content: Review priority, locate the relevant incident, open its details, and advance the next allowed workflow step. Retain the incident lifecycle, reporting, map, teams, escalations, notifications, analytics, administration and account capabilities. Label illustrative content as sample data. Never imply an external emergency call or unconfigured integration succeeded.

Unresolved decisions: Role-specific permissions for every route must match existing API authorization. Avoid showing unavailable admin or dispatch tools as broken pages. The responsive mobile navigation must remain keyboard-operable and preserve the current route.

## Direction contract

THESIS: Put the incident picture and immediate next decision before secondary reporting and administration controls; refuse the generic four-card dashboard as the page's main structure.

OWN-WORLD: A civic operations workspace with deep teal navigation, warm neutral work surfaces, restrained brick actions, plain semantic severity labels, concise humanist sans typography, thin dividers, and compact familiar controls.

STORY: Staff can see what needs attention, why it is prioritized, and where to act. Public users can report or track without entering staff-only tools.

FIRST VIEWPORT: On desktop, persistent role-grouped navigation and a slim scope-aware top bar frame the page. A clear overview heading leads to a horizontal situation summary and direct review action; compact totals follow, then the priority queue and a next-actions panel. On narrow screens, navigation becomes a labeled drawer, summary stacks above counts, and queue rows become readable incident summaries without page-wide horizontal scrolling.
